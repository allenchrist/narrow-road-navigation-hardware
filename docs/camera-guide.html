Measuring road width with your phone's camera

Your phone films the road ahead; a program on the laptop turns the picture into metres and tells the main unit how wide the road is. Nothing to wire and nothing to upload to an ESP32.

Tick each step as you finish it; your ticks are remembered in this browser. If a step doesn't turn out as described, stop there and send Claude what the terminal printed, or the picture calibration-check.png.

0

What you have, and what each part does

Android phone IP Webcam app

Films the road and sends the video over Wi-Fi. The same phone can also be the hotspot.

Laptop runs width.py

Reads the video, finds the two sides of the road in each picture, works out the width in metres and broadcasts it.

Main unit already done

Hears each width, places it on the map a few metres ahead of the car, and spots narrow stretches.

calibration.json made in stage 7

The camera's only setting: how pixels in the picture map onto metres on the ground.

You also need

  • A phone holder for the dashboard or windscreen that holds the phone firmly.
  • A tape measure (at least 5 m) and 4 markers: stones, bricks, chalk crosses or tape.
  • A phone charger for the car: the camera and hotspot together drain the battery fast.
  • A helper for the drive: someone other than the driver watches the laptop.
1

How it measures

Five times a second the laptop broadcasts on the hotspot, exactly like the other units:

{"type":"width","width":3.2,"wide":false,"distance":6.0,"confidence":1.0}

The main unit places each reading 6 m ahead of the car on the map. When readings say 4 m or less along at least 25 m (70% of the readings), it sends that stretch to the server as a narrow road suggestion, and the admin approves or rejects it on the website as usual.

Calibration
Teaching the program how big things are: you put 4 markers a known distance apart and click them in the picture.
Homography
The maths that turns a pixel into a point on flat ground. calibration.json holds it.
Confidence
0 to 1: how many of the three rows found both sides. 1.0 means all three agreed.
Wide
At least one side is outside the picture: wider than the camera can see.
2

Python on the laptop

5 minutes, once
  1. Check Python. In VS Code's terminal type python --version. It must say 3.10 or newer (yours has 3.13).
  2. Go to the camera folder: 
    cd D:\allen\narrow-road-hardware\narrow-road-hardware\camera-unit\python

    Every camera command in this guide runs from this folder.

  3. Install the two libraries (OpenCV for pictures, numpy for maths): 
    pip install -r requirements.txt

    Yours are already installed (OpenCV 4.12, numpy 2.2); running it again does no harm.

  4. Run the built-in test. 
    python tests/test_width.py

    It draws pretend lanes of 2.5 to 6 m and measures them. It should end with All passed, each lane within about 10 cm.

3

Set up the phone

5 minutes
  1. Install "IP Webcam" from the Play Store (by Pavel Khlebovich, free). An iPhone can use DroidCam instead: the address ends in :4747/video.
  2. Set the picture size. Open the app → Video preferences → Video resolution → 640×480.

    The program shrinks every picture to 320×240 anyway, so bigger is no better, just slower over Wi-Fi.

  3. Landscape. Video preferences → Video orientation → Landscape. The phone will lie sideways in its holder.
  4. Scroll to the bottom and tap "Start server".

    The camera picture appears with an address at the bottom, such as http://10.40.78.1:8080. Write it down: it's YOUR-PHONE in the commands below.

Keep the screen from switching off

In IP Webcam's Power management, turn on "Prevent phone from going to sleep". Otherwise the video stops when the screen turns off.

4

Hotspot and a test picture

  1. Turn on the phone's hotspot, the same one the main unit uses.
  2. Connect the laptop to that hotspot.

    This matters: the laptop must be on the same hotspot as the ESP32s, or the widths never reach them. Your home Wi-Fi won't work for this.

  3. Open YOUR-PHONE's address in Chrome on the laptop, for example http://10.40.78.1:8080. IP Webcam's page shows the live picture. If it does, the laptop can see the phone.

    If the address changed after you turned on the hotspot, use the new one the app shows.

  4. The first time you run a camera command, Windows may ask whether Python can use the network. Tick Private networks and click Allow.
5

Try it at home first

15 minutes, optional

A corridor, a passage between two walls, or a driveway works as a small "road". Because it's smaller, use a smaller marker rectangle and measure closer:

  1. Hold the phone about 1 m high, on a stack of books or a tripod, looking along the corridor and slightly down.
  2. Put 4 markers on the floor: 1 m apart side to side, at 2 m and 4 m from the phone, centred in front of it.
  3. Calibrate for that rectangle (1 m wide, from 2 to 4 m): 
    python calibrate.py --source http://YOUR-PHONE:8080/video --rect 1,2,4 --out home.json

    Click the markers as in stage 7, then press Enter.

  4. Measure 2.5 to 3.5 m away: 
    python width.py --source http://YOUR-PHONE:8080/video --calibration home.json --distances 2.5,3,3.5

    Compare the number with the corridor's real width from the tape measure. Press q in the window to stop.

This uses its own file, home.json, so it doesn't spoil the car's calibration later.

6

Mount the phone in the car

  1. Centred, sideways, firm. In the holder, in the middle of the windscreen or dashboard, so the camera is in line with the middle of the car.
  2. Looking straight ahead and slightly down. On IP Webcam's screen, the road 4 to 8 m ahead should fill the lower half of the picture, and the bonnet should only just show, if at all.
  3. Wipe the windscreen in front of the lens, and keep the driver's view clear.
Once calibrated, it mustn't move

Even a small knock changes which pixel is 6 m ahead. If the phone moves, calibrate again (stage 7). A mark on the holder helps you put it back in the same place.

7

Calibrate with markers

15 minutes, once
  1. Park on flat, open ground (an empty car park, a quiet road) and place the 4 markers as in the picture.

    Find the point on the ground directly under the phone. Measure 4 m and 8 m straight ahead from it, along the car's centre line. At each distance, put one marker 1 m to the left and one 1 m to the right. Markers that stand out from the ground (white on dark tarmac) are easiest to click.

  2. Run the calibration from the camera-unit\python folder: 
    python calibrate.py --source http://YOUR-PHONE:8080/video
  3. Click the 4 markers in order: near-left, near-right, far-right, far-left. The window says which one is next and puts a red cross on each. Made a mistake? Press r to start again.
  4. Press Enter. It prints: 
    Saved calibration.json
...
On this picture: more than 5.40 m wide
Saved calibration-check.png with the measurement marked

    The last lines are its first measurement, on the calibration picture itself. Open calibration-check.png: red dots should sit on the sides of the road.

Ignore the line about CAM_IMAGE_TO_GROUND: that's for an ESP32-CAM, not the phone.

8

Run the measuring

  1. Start it: 
    python width.py --source http://YOUR-PHONE:8080/video

    It prints Measuring; sending to <broadcast>:4210, then a line every second:

    3.12 m  confidence 1.00  NARROW
>6.40 m  confidence 0.67  wide
no reading: is the road 5-7 m ahead in view?
  2. Read the window. It shows the picture with:
    • a green dot: the middle of the road, where each scan starts
    • red dots: the sides it found
    • orange dots: the scan reached the edge of the picture (road wider than the view)
    • the width at the top: amber when 4 m or less (narrow), green otherwise
  3. Keys: s saves the current picture as road-….png (useful for your report and for asking Claude about a bad reading); q stops.
While driving, no window

Add --no-window to save the laptop's battery once you trust it: python width.py --source http://YOUR-PHONE:8080/video --no-window. Press Ctrl + C to stop.

9

Check the main unit hears it

  1. Power the main unit and open its Monitor (PlatformIO → main-unit → esp32dev → Monitor). Its status line now ends with: 
    [Status] server: connected VEHICLE_009 | GPS: fix, 8 sats ... | camera: 3.1 m, 0s ago

    >6.4 m means wide. If there's no camera: part, the laptop isn't on the same hotspot.

  2. A narrow stretch found while driving shows as: 
    [Narrow] Found a narrow road: 3.2 m wide along 31 m
[Server] Narrow road suggested; review it on the admin page

    Open the website's admin page to approve or reject it.

It needs a GPS fix

The main unit only places widths on the map while its GPS has a fix. Indoors, the camera part still prints, but no suggestions are made.

Measure its accuracy (for your report)

Stop in 10 to 15 different lanes. In each, measure the real width with the tape, at about 6 m in front of the car, and write down what the camera says (press s to keep the picture).

#PlaceTape (m)Camera (m)Error (m)
1e.g. lane behind college3.103.02−0.08

Average error = the average of the error column, ignoring minus signs. "Within 15 cm on 12 of 15 lanes" is the kind of result to report. Note where it went wrong (shadows, parked bikes, painted lines): that's useful too.

11

Every time after the first setup

  1. Phone: hotspot on, phone in its holder in the same place, IP Webcam → Start server.
  2. Laptop: join the hotspot, then in VS Code's terminal: 
    cd D:\allen\narrow-road-hardware\narrow-road-hardware\camera-unit\python
python width.py --source http://YOUR-PHONE:8080/video
  3. Main unit: give it power. It hears the camera within a second.

No calibration again unless the phone's position changed.

13

Limits and safety

The driver never touches the laptop or phone

A passenger watches the readings. Set everything up before you drive, and mount the phone where it doesn't block the driver's view.

What it does well, and where it struggles

SituationHow it does
Narrow lanes between walls or buildings (the main use)Good: within about 10 cm in tests
A tree shadow across part of the roadGood: it uses the middle of three rows, so one shadowed row doesn't matter
Wide open roadsSays "wide", which is right: they're not narrow
Wide roads with painted white linesWeak: it can take the line for the road's side and read too narrow (a 7 m road read as 4.4 to 5.2 m in tests). The main unit's 25 m rule and the admin review catch most of these
Parked vehicles, people, animals in the laneReads too narrow while they're in view
Night, heavy rain, fog, glare from a low sunUnreliable: use it in daylight
A lane edged by open ground the same colour as the roadHard to see the side: may read wide

These are worth stating honestly in your report, next to your accuracy table.
